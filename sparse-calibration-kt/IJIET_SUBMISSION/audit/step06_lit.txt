Classical approaches include Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT), which tracks a latent mastery state for each skill [1], and item-response theory (IRT), of which the one-parameter Rasch model is the canonical form used in this study [6]. [Heading 2] Graph and self-supervised KT
[Text] Graph-based KT (GKT) represents knowledge components as nodes of a graph and updates proficiency with a graph neural network [8]. Contrastive learning for KT (CL4KT) trains representations from augmented views of learning histories [9]. These models are related architectures, not the contribution of the present paper. They are included later only as an exploratory single-fold diagnostic on ASSISTments 2012, not as a proposed method and not as a state-of-the-art comparison.

[Heading 2] Calibration and educational decision support
